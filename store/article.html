How a honeybee finds its way home

A foraging bee can fly several kilometres from the hive and still return in a straight line. It does this without a map, a landmark it has seen before, or any sense of the path it took. Instead, it keeps a running tally of every turn and every stretch of flight.

Scientists call this path integration. The bee measures distance by how quickly the ground seems to slide past its eyes, and it measures direction against the position of the sun. Because the sun moves across the sky during the day, the bee also corrects for the time that has passed. When it is ready to go home, it simply flies the reverse of the sum of its journey.

Back at the hive, the bee shares what it found. In the dark, on the vertical face of the comb, it performs a short waggle dance. The angle of the dance tells the other bees which way to fly relative to the sun. The length of each waggle tells them how far.
